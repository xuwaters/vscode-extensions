## Khronos Group — OpenGL Shading Language reference pages

`wasm/wgsl_lsp_wasm_bg.wasm` embeds documentation for GLSL's builtin functions
and variables — the description shown on hover, and the note shown for each
parameter. That prose is Khronos Group reference-page material, obtained from
[docs.gl](https://docs.gl) ([github.com/BSVino/docs.gl](https://github.com/BSVino/docs.gl)),
which republishes the Khronos OpenGL Shading Language reference pages.

> Copyright © 2011-2014 Khronos Group. This material may be distributed subject
> to the terms and conditions set forth in the Open Publication License, v 1.0,
> 8 June 1999. <https://opencontent.org/openpub/>

**Modified**, as the Open Publication License requires modified versions to say.
The pages were converted from XHTML to Markdown, stripped of boilerplate,
truncated to a per-entry budget, and had their formulas, tables and images
replaced with a note pointing back at the reference page. The prototypes and
version tables the same pages carry are the language's own syntax and facts
about it, and are used as such.

The original pages are at <https://docs.gl>; the conversion is
`crates/wgsl-shader/glsl-spec-gen` in this repository, and the exact docs.gl
commit it read is named in the header of every file under
`crates/wgsl-shader/glsl-spec/src/generated/`.

The docs.gl scaffolding that reshaped the Khronos pages is placed in the public
domain by its author, per that repository's `readme.md`.

The Open Publication License is a documentation licence, not an OSI-approved
software licence. It covers the embedded prose only. That is why `glsl-spec`, the
crate that carries it, is declared `MIT AND OPUBL-1.0` — MIT for its code, the
Open Publication License for this prose — and why the licence's full text is
among those reproduced below.

[vscode-wgsl](https://github.com/PolyMeilex/vscode-wgsl), which is itself built

block-comment rules, the operator patterns and their comments, and the order in
